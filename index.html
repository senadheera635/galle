Galle Fort

Galle Fort is an extremely important and historical location situated on the southern coast of Sri Lanka, designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is considered one of the best-preserved historical fortifications in the world today. The fort was originally built by the Portuguese in 1588, and later in the 17th century (from 1640 onwards) by the Dutch, it was further expanded into its massive current structure. It features a unique blend of European architectural elements and South Asian cultural features, with its cobblestone narrow streets and ancient buildings remaining unchanged to this day.

The Galle Lighthouse

The Galle Lighthouse stands as one of Sri Lanka's most iconic and photographed landmarks, gracefully positioned at the southeastern tip of the historic Galle Fort. Originally built by the British in 1848, the current structure was reconstructed in 1939 after a devastating fire destroyed the original tower. Soaring about 18 meters (nearly 60 feet) high and painted a striking, pristine white against the deep blue backdrop of the Indian Ocean, it is recognized as Sri Lanka's oldest light station. Framed by stone ramparts, swaying coconut palms, and crashing waves, the lighthouse is not only a functional beacon guiding ships safely along the southern coast but also a beloved gathering spot where locals and international visitors alike come to watch breathtaking sunsets..


The Ramparts and Bastions

The Ramparts and Bastions of Galle Fort are important defensive structures built to protect the fort and its harbour from attacks. The ramparts are large, strong walls surrounding the fort, while the bastions are projecting sections of the walls that provided strategic positions for soldiers and cannons. Some famous bastions include the Sun Bastion, Moon Bastion, Star Bastion, and Point Utrecht Bastion. Today, the ramparts are popular among visitors because they offer beautiful views of the Indian Ocean, Galle Lighthouse, and the surrounding area, especially at sunset. These structures are an important example of the military architecture and historical heritage of Galle Fort.


Clock Tower & Historic Churches

The Galle Fort Clock Tower, built in 1883 on the Sun Bastion, is an iconic 85-foot landmark erected to honor the respected colonial surgeon Dr. Peter Daniel Anthonisz. Situated near the main entrance, this historic structure overlooks the charming cobblestone streets and offers visitors a brilliant vantage point of the inner fortress city.

Adding to the fort's rich religious heritage are its remarkable historic churches, most notably the Dutch Reformed Church (Groote Kerk) constructed in 1755. As one of the oldest Protestant churches in continuous use in Sri Lanka, it features a unique colonial design, traditional stained-glass windows, and a floor paved with fascinating Dutch-era gravestones.


A Living Community

Galle Fort is not just a collection of ancient monuments and museums; it is celebrated worldwide as a living community. Unlike many other historical fortifications that serve purely as tourist sites or museums, hundreds of people continue to live, work, and raise families within its stone walls, preserving a unique multicultural lifestyle that spans centuries

Walking through its historic, cobblestone streets, you will see a vibrant blend of daily local life and modern boutique culture. Traditional family homes, local grocery shops, schools, and cricket-loving children playing on the ramparts share space with trendy art galleries, boutique hotels, stylish cafes, and international designer stores. This harmonious coexistence of the past and the present gives Galle Fort its rare, breathing soul, making every visit feel like stepping into a living storybook.
